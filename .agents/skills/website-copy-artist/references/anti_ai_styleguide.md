# The Anti-AI Copywriting Styleguide

## Why Most AI Copy Fails to Convert
Large language models naturally gravitate toward the statistical mean of their training data. This produces:
- Generic, breathless hype ("We help you unleash your full potential!").
- Overuse of metaphorical padding ("a testament to innovation", "navigating the complex landscape").
- False enthusiasm that makes real human buyers immediately cringe and bounce.

To convert high-value clients, executives, and founders, copy must sound like it was written by an elite practitioner with scar tissue, real-world experience, and taste.

---

## The Rule of Concrete Particulars
Whenever you feel the urge to write a general claim, replace it with a specific, tangible detail.

| Generic AI Copy | Elite Conversion Artist Copy |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Our solutions are fast and reliable." | "Sub-12ms API latency with 99.99% uptime SLA backed by multi-region Redis replicas." |
| "We help companies save money." | "Cut $1.8M in annual manual dispatch overhead within 3 weeks of production cutover." |
| "Experience seamless workflow collaboration." | "Zero messy Slack handovers. One unified, real-time lead board that syncs instantly to PostgreSQL." |
| "Modern, state-of-the-art designs." | "Swiss typographic grid, responsive touch viewports, and sub-second TTFB on Next.js 15." |

## Cadence and Sentence Architecture (The Music of Prose)

Gary Provost's classic lesson on sentence length:

> *"This sentence has five words. Here are five words more. Five-word sentences are fine. But several together become monotonous. Listen to what is happening. The writing is getting boring. The sound of it drones. It’s like a stuck record. The ear demands some variety.*
>
> *Now listen. I vary the sentence length, and I create music. Music. The writing sings. It has a pleasant rhythm, a lilt, a harmony. I use short sentences. And I use sentences of medium length. And sometimes, when I see the reader is rested, I will engage him with a sentence of considerable length, a sentence that burns with energy and builds with all the impetus of a crescendo, the roll of the drums, the crash of the cymbals–sounds that say listen to this, it is important."*

### Application for Landing Pages
- **Hero Statement**: Punchy, declarative, 8–10 words.
- **Support Paragraph**: First sentence establishes reality. Second sentence delivers the proof.
- **Button Microcopy**: 2–4 words maximum with high intent and zero ambiguity.

## Psychological Friction Reducers

When visitors reach a call-to-action, they experience **micro-anxieties**:
1. *"Will they spam my inbox?"* ➔ Add: *"Zero spam. Only senior technical architects respond."*
2. *"Is this going to be an aggressive sales pitch?"* ➔ Add: *"A 20-minute scoping discussion, not a pitch."*
3. *"What if the project blows past budget?"* ➔ Add: *"Fixed milestone escrow. You never pay for scope creep."*
4. *"Do I own the code?"* ➔ Add: *"100% IP & repository ownership transferred to you upon launch."*
5. *"How fast do you respond?"* ➔ Add: *"Sub-2 hour direct response time."*

## The "So What?" Test
Every time you draft a headline, bullet point, or feature card, ask:
**"So what?"**

- *Draft:* "Engineered on Next.js 15 and Supabase."
- *So what?* "Your site loads in 0.38 seconds."
- *So what?* "Mobile shoppers don't abandon their carts, boosting checkout conversion by 48%."
- *Final Copy:* **"0.38s Page Load. 48% Higher Mobile Conversions. Engineered on Next.js 15."**
